# Office joke candidates

Scores are subjective editorial estimates, not judge results. Each criterion is
scored from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong). Priority follows the judge's order: office
one-liner fit, laugh/punchline, cold-read clarity, freshness, then tight wording.

| # | Candidate | Fit | Laugh | Clarity | Freshness | Tightness |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | We replaced the suggestion box with a shredder for transparency. | 5 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 5 |
| 2 | I put my resignation in the shared doc and three people left comments asking for edits. | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 4 |
| 3 | I told my manager I needed a mental health day; she scheduled it for Q3 and sent a pre-read. | 5 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 3 |
| 4 | I set my status to 'Do Not Disturb' and my manager booked a meeting titled 'Quick question about your status'. | 5 | 3 | 5 | 3 | 3 |
| 5 | HR made the anonymous survey mandatory, then emailed me a reminder to be less specific. | 5 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| 6 | Our office escape room was canceled after HR called it a retention risk. | 5 | 3 | 5 | 4 | 5 |
| 7 | HR renamed layoffs 'alumni onboarding' and assigned me a buddy. | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 5 |

Candidates 1–4 were supplied in the task; 5–7 were drafted in this session.

## Selection

Candidate 2 wins. The shared document is a specific, familiar office object;
coworkers treating a resignation as another collaborative draft supplies a clear
reversal. Candidate 1's shredder/suggestion-box pairing is familiar and its
transparency claim takes an extra interpretive step. Candidate 3 piles on two
bureaucratic beats. Candidate 4 mostly restates an interruption. Candidate 5 uses
the familiar non-anonymous survey premise. Candidate 6 has a clean pun but a less
believable scene. Candidate 7 has a sharp euphemism, but the buddy adds less than
the resignation's edits and requires interpreting the onboarding parody.

## Final refinement

I put my resignation in the shared doc; three coworkers requested edits.

The semicolon replaces the connective. “Coworkers” establishes who is commenting;
“requested edits” removes the redundant explanation of how document comments
work. The final word carries the reversal. This is the fixed text in main.ts.
